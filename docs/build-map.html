01 · Why

The problem and the goal

Problem

Designing a space mission is a chain of trade-offs: a better instrument adds mass, power, data and cost. Students rarely get to try a whole mission, so these trade-offs stay abstract.

Goal

Players design a full mission on a 3D island, launch it, fly it, and on the Moon drive a rover into a dark crater. An AI mission controller (CAPCOM) coaches them and explains every outcome, using only the simulation's numbers.

Golden rule

The simulation decides. The AI explains.

Every number, score and outcome comes from @mi/sim, our deterministic engine. Claude only chooses words, asks the engine questions through tools, and picks events from a safe list. If the AI is slow or down, scripted text takes over and the game still works.

Science goal = how you win

06 · Design rules

Six resources, one launch check

Every choice moves several resources at once. A high-resolution telescope adds mass, power use, cost and data, all from one click.

Masskg

Must fit the bus's carrying limit and the rocket's capacity to the chosen orbit.

PowerW

Average power made must cover what everything uses, including in shadow.

Budget$M

Hardware + 10% assembly and testing + launch + operations per year.

DataGb / day

How much the instruments produce each day.

CommsGb / day

How much the antenna and ground stations can send home.

Timeyears + days

Mission lifetime plus cruise time to far destinations.

flowchart LR
  D[Player's design] --> C{Design review}
  C -- any blocking issue --> F[Must fix before launch]
  F --> D
  C -- only warnings --> W[Can launch, but the mission will suffer]
  C -- all clear --> G[Launch]
  W --> G

Blocking issues: must fix

  • Every subsystem chosen, at least one instrument
  • Payload within the bus's carrying limit
  • Rocket can reach the orbit and lift the whole spacecraft
  • Engine has enough Δv to enter orbit at the Moon, Mars, GEO or L1
  • Total cost within budget
  • Team mode: every player has signed off (any change resets it)

Warnings: allowed, but they hurt

  • Not enough power: instruments switch off part of the time
  • Battery too small for shadow: instruments go dark, or worse
  • Antenna too slow: extra data is lost
  • Pointing too coarse: blurry observations
  • Orbit doesn't suit the objective
  • Low orbit without thrusters: falls after about 2 years

Exploration bonus: 8 gold crystals are hidden on the island. Each adds $3M to the budget and shows a real space fact.

07 · Flight rules

What happens every simulated day

Before flight, development runs 3–15% over cost (paid from the reserve) and the rocket can fail at its real reliability (93–98%). Then the engine steps through the mission one day at a time.

flowchart TD
  A[New day] --> B{Still cruising?}
  B -- yes --> N[Next day]
  B -- no --> P[Power made today: solar fades 2.5% a year]
  P --> I[Instruments run as much as power and battery allow]
  I --> D[Data produced]
  D --> L{Can the antenna send it all?}
  L -- yes --> S[Science added]
  L -- no --> X[Most valuable data sent first, the rest is lost]
  X --> S
  S --> H{Radiation fault, flat battery or air drag?}
  H -- yes --> E[Mission ends early]
  H -- no --> EV{Event due today?}
  EV -- yes --> C[Team picks one of two choices]
  C --> N
  EV -- no --> N
  N --> A

08 · Shadowline rules

The Moon rover phase

After the orbiter's mission, the player lands a rover near the south pole and plans a route into a shadowed crater to find ice, then back out to sunlight.

Choices made on the island

Rover
Small (350 kg with lander, 1,500 Wh) or VIPER-class (1,100 kg, 4,500 Wh, drill)
Relay
None (Earth only) · Orbiter relay (short passes every 2 h) · Rim mast (constant where visible)
Site
2–3 landing sites: closer to ice vs more sunlight and Earth view

The rules on the surface

  • In shadow: no charging, and heaters must run
  • No view of Earth or a relay: data stays on the rover
  • Battery at zero: rover lost, with all unsent data
  • Slope over 25°: the rover gets stuck
  • Only data that reaches Earth scores

The step-by-step logic is drawn in the Shadowline diagram further down (section on how the Moon rover phase works).

09 · Science index

How science is measured

The science index measures how much useful science actually reached Earth. Each day, every instrument adds points; the total is averaged over the planned mission, so a spacecraft that dies early scores less.

Free assets

  • NASA: 3D spacecraft models, Blue Marble, Moon Kit, Moon Trek
  • Poly Haven: HDRIs and textures (CC0)
  • Kenney, Quaternius: props and characters (CC0)
  • Sketchfab only after checking each license

AI (only where it is safe)

  • World Labs: backdrop scenes
  • Text-to-3D tools: small props, then cleanup in Blender
  • AI images: skybox, screen graphics
  • Never for anything the simulation depends on

Order of work: biggest visual gain first

  1. Lighting and post-processingHDRI sky, colour grading, bloom. About a day; every scene looks better at once.
  2. Real planetsSwap procedural textures for NASA maps in the orbit view.
  3. Spacecraft materialsGold foil, metal and glass that reflect the sky.
  4. Launch cinematicCamera cuts, smoke, darkening sky. Hero shot 1 for the video.
  5. Moon craterReal terrain, low Sun, headlights. Hero shot 2.
  6. Buildings from BlenderReplace simple boxes with modeled buildings.
  7. Props and astronautCC0 packs, or AI text-to-3D with cleanup.
  8. AI backdropOnly if time is left.

3D rules

  • The simulation decides; 3D only shows. Crater shadows come from the same sunlight calculation the game uses.
  • Fast: compress every model; 60 fps on a mid-range laptop; shadows and bloom turn off on slow machines.
  • Licensed: keep a list of every model, texture and map with its license and credit, for the project page.
  • No NASA logos or insignia on our models.
  • Original: learn from Red Frontier's method (scripted Blender scenes exported as .glb), but don't reuse their art.

About world models

AI that makes 3D scenes to export and keep (like World Labs) is a good tool for scenery. AI that streams live video worlds is not used: it can't follow our simulation's numbers, costs money every minute, and needs a live connection during the demo.

18 · Shadowline

How the Moon rover phase works

Real NASA Moon Trek elevation data is turned into tiles once, offline. During play, every 15 simulated minutes the engine asks two questions: can the rover see the Sun, and can it see Earth or a relay?

flowchart TD
  DEM[Moon Trek elevation, south pole] --> PY[Python pipeline: crop, resample, horizon maps]
  PY --> BLOB[Tiles in Vercel Blob]
  BLOB --> POS[Rover position on real terrain]
  POS --> SUN{Can it see the Sun?}
  SUN -- yes --> CH[Solar panels charge the battery]
  SUN -- no --> DR[Battery drains, heaters on]
  POS --> EA{Can it see Earth or the relay?}
  EA -- yes --> TX[Ice data sent home]
  EA -- no --> HOLD[Data stays on the rover]
  DR --> EMPTY{Battery empty?}
  EMPTY -- yes --> LOST[Rover lost, unsent data lost]
  EMPTY -- no --> NEXT[Drive to next waypoint]
  CH --> NEXT
  TX --> NEXT
  HOLD --> NEXT
  NEXT --> POS

The 3D crater only shows what the engine computed: sunlit ground is lit, and the signal line turns red when contact is lost. The surprising failure comes from the red branches: the rover finds the ice, then dies before its data gets home.

26 · Glossary

Words you will hear

CAPCOM
The "capsule communicator": the one person in mission control who talks to the crew. Our AI's character.
CML
Concept Maturity Level, NASA/JPL's 1–4 ladder from idea to point design. Our quest steps follow it.
Δv (delta-v)
How much the engine can change the spacecraft's speed, in m/s. Needed to enter orbit around the Moon or Mars.
LEO, SSO, GEO, L1
Orbit types: low Earth, sun-synchronous, geostationary, and the Sun–Earth balance point.
Downlink
How much data per day the spacecraft can send home. Data that does not fit is lost.
DEM
Digital elevation model: a grid of terrain heights. We use one from NASA Moon Trek.
Horizon map
For each spot on the terrain, how high the land rises in each direction. Tells us if the Sun or Earth is visible.
Deterministic
Same design + same seed always gives the same result. Lets us test and verify scores.
Tool call
When Claude asks our code to run something (like what_if) and uses the result in its answer.
Fallback
Scripted text or logic used when the AI is slow, down, or over its limit.
R3F
React Three Fiber: writing Three.js 3D scenes as React components.
Web Worker
A background thread in the browser, so heavy math does not freeze the 3D view.
